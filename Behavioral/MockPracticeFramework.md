# Behavioral Mock Interview Practice Framework

## Purpose

A structured way to **practice** behavioral interviews - solo, with a peer, and with a professional
mock. This file is the *practice process*. For the underlying content, use the canonical guides:
- **STAR method, question categories, common mistakes** → [Behavioral Guide](BehavioralGuide.md)
- **Your story bank** → [STAR Stories](STAR-stories.md)
- **Company frameworks** → [company-specific/](company-specific/)
- **Overall timeline** → [10-Week Study Plan](../InterviewPrep/StudyPlan-10weeks.md)

> This doc deliberately does **not** re-teach STAR or re-list the question bank - those live in the
> guides above. It focuses on *how to practice and improve*.

---

## 1️⃣ Solo Practice (record yourself)

The single highest-ROI solo drill: **say the story out loud and record it** (phone is fine). Reading
silently hides all the problems.

1. Pick a story from your [story bank](STAR-stories.md).
2. Say it out loud, unscripted. Time it.
3. Watch the recording and check:
   - Over 3 minutes? → cut context.
   - Vague ("we improved things")? → add numbers/tools/names.
   - Unclear what *you* did? → convert "we" to "I" in the Action.
   - No result/learning? → add impact + takeaway.
4. Re-record until it feels natural (not memorized).

**One story, many questions.** The same "production outage you fixed" story can answer *solved a
complex problem*, *worked under pressure*, *took ownership*, and *walk me through your debugging
process*. Practice re-framing one story for 4 different prompts - it's how you cover more with fewer
stories.

---

## 2️⃣ Handling Follow-Ups (the part most people skip)

Strong interviewers drill into your stories. This is where under-prepared candidates fall apart, so
practice it explicitly. For your top stories, be ready for:

| Follow-up | Weak answer | Strong answer |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| "Why did you make that decision?" | "It seemed right." | "I chose A over B because X; I weighed trade-offs Y vs Z." |
| "What would you do differently?" | "Nothing." | "I'd do X earlier, because Y." (shows growth, not defensiveness) |
| "How did the team react?" | "They did what I said." | "Initially skeptical; after I showed data on X, they bought in." |
| "What did you learn?" | "To communicate better." | "Specifically Z - and here's how I applied it on a later project." |

Pre-load, for each of your ~5-6 core stories: the **alternatives you rejected**, the **constraints**
that shaped the decision, and what you'd **change with hindsight**.

---

## 3️⃣ Peer Practice - 45-minute session

Find a partner (fellow job seeker, coworker prepping for promo, friend in tech).

- **0-20 min:** Partner A interviews B - 2-3 behavioral questions + follow-ups.
- **20-25 min:** Feedback (see rubric below).
- **25-45 min:** Swap roles.

**Feedback rubric (score 1-5 each), give one concrete example per score:**

| Criteria | 1 (Poor) | 5 (Excellent) |
|----------|----------|---------------|
| Structure | no clear STAR | clean, easy-to-follow STAR |
| Specificity | vague | concrete numbers/tools/names |
| Your role | unclear what YOU did | crystal-clear individual contribution |
| Result | no outcome | quantified impact + learning |
| Length / delivery | <1 or >4 min, rehearsed | 2-3 min, conversational |

Target: average 4+ before you interview for real. (This same rubric works for scoring your own
recordings in step 1.)

---

## 4️⃣ Professional Mock (after 2-3 weeks of the above)

Services: **Pramp** / **interviewing.io** (free), **Exponent** / **Pathrise** / a Fiverr coach (paid).
Expect 45-60 min, several behavioral questions with follow-ups, and written feedback.

**After the mock:** note the *patterns* the interviewer flagged (e.g. "you drift into 'we'"), rewrite
1-2 weak stories, re-practice them out loud, and book another mock a week later to measure improvement.

---

## 5️⃣ Post-Interview Reflection (do this after every real interview)

Capturing this while it's fresh is what compounds across a job search:

```
Questions asked:        [list them - build your personal question bank]
Stories I used:         [story -> question mapping]
Follow-ups asked:       [the drill-downs you got]
What went well:         [specifics]
What to improve:        [specifics]
Signals I noticed:      [what the interviewer seemed to care about]
Prep before next round: [weak story to rewrite, topic to review]
```

## Readiness Checklist

- [ ] 12-15 polished STAR stories (see [STAR Stories](STAR-stories.md)), covering all categories
- [ ] Each core story survives follow-up drilling (alternatives / constraints / hindsight ready)
- [ ] Practiced out loud and recorded - not just in your head
- [ ] 2-3 peer mocks + 1-2 professional mocks done, feedback iterated
- [ ] Target company's framework reviewed ([company-specific/](company-specific/))
- [ ] Questions ready to ask the interviewer ([Questions to Ask](QuestionsToAsk.md))

**You're ready. Good luck!** 🚀
